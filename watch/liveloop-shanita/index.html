Your December books decide how your tax season feels. Every January I meet business owners who are stressed about one thing: twelve months of transactions nobody has looked at since spring. This week I'm sharing the year-end bookkeeping cleanup I walk my clients through. Small steps, done now, so you start the new year with clear numbers and confident moves. Step one is tomorrow. Save this so you can follow along.

#SWAccountingSolutions #BookkeepingTips #YearEndCleanup #SmallBusinessOwner

Tuesday · 12:00 PMPost

Step one of a year-end cleanup: reconcile every account. Bank accounts. Credit cards. Loans. PayPal, Square, Stripe. If money moved through it, it should match your books through December 31. Reconciling is where we catch the duplicate charge, the deposit that never got recorded and the subscription you forgot you were paying for. It isn't glamorous. It's the foundation everything else stands on.

#Bookkeeping #YearEndCleanup #SmallBusinessFinance #SWAccountingSolutions

Wednesday · 9:00 AMStory video (script only)

Three things I want every business owner to do before December 31. One: separate personal and business. If they're mixed together, untangle them now, not in April. Two: collect a W-9 from every contractor you paid this year, so your 1099s go out on time in January. Three: follow up on your unpaid invoices. Money you're owed is easier to collect in December than in March. Clear numbers. Confident moves. I'm Shanita Wilkerson with SW Accounting Solutions.

Story video script · about 45 seconds, starring you

Thursday · 12:00 PMPost

"Uncategorized" is not a category. Before the year closes, go through every transaction sitting in uncategorized or "ask my accountant." Give each one a home, and match it to a receipt. Your future self, and your tax preparer, will thank you. Clean categories mean every deduction you've earned is easy to find, and nothing gets guessed.

#BookkeepingTips #SmallBusinessOwner #YearEnd #SWAccountingSolutions
